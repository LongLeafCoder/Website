On making a place you can return to

The best personal systems feel less like rules and more like familiar rooms.

A workbench teaches you what a homepage rarely can: every tool earns its place by being used. The surface gathers marks, the edges soften, and the small arrangement of things begins to hold a memory of the work.

I think a personal practice needs that same kind of room. Not a perfect system, but a place that makes it easier to begin again.

For me, that place is somewhere between the shop and the screen, between the grain of a board and the blank page. The work becomes more honest when both sides are allowed to inform each other.
